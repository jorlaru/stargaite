# Phase 5 design

Status: the design phase 5 is built from, the first phase after v1 (phases 1 to 4 of kick-off §20). It builds on the code at `af8796c` and on `docs/design/phase-1.md` to `docs/design/phase-4.md` (cited as "phase-N §M"). Its inputs are a review of the shipped code and a comparison run of 473 calls against a live deployment and LiteLLM (2026-11-16), cited only as "the comparison run"; every finding this design acts on is stated here in its own words, with its evidence. Rule ids (PAR, RSN, TEL, USE, LIM, RES, SEC, OPS, PUB, ADR, AC, ASM, OQ, R) are the kick-off's and binding; where one must change, §17 gives the exact wording and the design assumes it. Marks: **measured** is a Go program or test run on 2026-11-16 in the pinned `golang:1.27.4-trixie` image, without network, on a copy of the repository carrying a prototype; **measured (review)** is a figure measured during the review, by the same means, that this design did not run again; **derived** is a reading of the code; **docs** is a provider's or SDK's public documentation, not reproduced. ## 1. Scope and outcome

Phase 5 adds no endpoint and no feature area. Its purpose is the project's: remove friction between clients, the proxy and providers while staying small and auditable. The findings come down to a few root causes, and every recurring friction among them is knowledge kept in the wrong place, or read in more than one place:

| Knowledge | Where it lives at `af8796c` | Where it belongs |
|---|---|---|
| What a provider or one of its models accepts beyond the reference, and refuses | Extra keys only in alias rules (`internal/domain/document.go:178`); refusals on the target, their remedy on every alias (`internal/plan/plan.go:439-454`) | Declared once on the provider, overridden on the target, read by one predicate in `plan` (§4) |
| How one model spells the output cap | On the target, seeded from the provider's `target_defaults` when `targets add` or `models add` creates it (`internal/admin/targets.go:44-56`, OPS-3), but applied to alias caps only (`plan.go:358-374`) | On the target, as today, and applied to every cap, the client's included (§4) |
| How slow a provider may be | The instance only (`document.go:41`, read at `internal/api/chat.go:508`, `:742`) | The provider, member by member, the setting as default (§4) |
| Whether a provider answer is an answer, and who must act on a failure | The status line alone (`chat.go:637-684`); a `2xx` body is a success once it is a JSON object (`internal/adapters/openai/openai.go:28-68`, `internal/adapters/anthropic/native.go:28-70`) | One effective status, one table by who must act, every consequence read from it (§5) |
| What a chat member means toward Messages | Judged key by key, three values by bytes (`internal/adapters/anthropic/anthropic.go:88-91`), nested members by ad-hoc lists (`:162-166`, `:287-289`) | One rule with five named outcomes inside the adapter (§6) |
| When a request has ended | For the proxy, after `done` releases the slots (`chat.go:952-956`); for a streaming client, at the last flushed event (`chat.go:722-725`), earlier | One moment for both paths (§7) |
| Whether a figure was reported | The record keeps `NULL`; `usage show` and `usage_daily` turn it into 0 (`internal/usage/show.go:74`, `internal/usage/writer.go:299-306`) | `NULL` at every level (§9) |

The rule of ownership this design adds:

> **What a provider accepts beyond the reference, refuses, or needs in time is a fact about every model it serves: it is declared on the provider and may be overridden on the target. What one model spells its own way (its reasoning dialect, its output-cap name) stays on the target, seeded at creation from the provider's `target_defaults` (OPS-3). The alias keeps the client's contract: effort, cap, requires, and its policy for keys its targets state nothing about or list in `unsupported_params`. A key that carries a contractual feature is never removed by a provider or a target: a target that cannot honour the feature declares the capability `false` (PAR-4).** Everything the proxy adds, removes or changes stays listed (PAR-3); there is no global "drop what is not supported" switch and no wildcard (R6).
>
> The line between the two inheritances is the scope of the fact: a fact true of every model behind one account resolves live from the provider, so one declaration serves every alias and every target; a fact that differs between models of one provider is copied once and then belongs to the target, so a correction of the provider's suggestion never rewrites a target's declared behaviour behind the operator's back.

At the end of phase 5:

- A provider or a target declares, once, a key it accepts beyond the reference (`forward`) or one it does not accept (`drop`); one predicate decides each key toward each target; a key that no rule of the alias, its targets or their providers names is still `400 unknown_parameter` before any call (C43).
- A client's cap reaches a target that declares `output_cap_param` under that name; a provider may declare its own timers (C43, C44).
- Every provider attempt has one effective status, the status line or the status an error object states in place of an answer; one table by who must act gives fallback, breaker, client answer, operator log and record, and `route`, which sees every attempt, gives the retry hint. A provider out of credit, refusing the key, or no longer serving the model hands the request to the alias's next target (C45).
- The chat→Anthropic adapter judges every member it reads by one rule with five named outcomes and removes nothing silently (C46).
- The end of a stream reaches the client when the request has ended for the proxy, as a synchronous answer already does (C47).
- The outbound pool fits a proxy's traffic (C48); a figure nobody reported is absent at every level, in the daily aggregates and in the cost of a call nobody answered (C49); a large body is scanned without being copied (C50).
- The operator's tools read the data plane's own sources, and the Messages reference is refreshed as R7 asks of each release (C51).

**Paid calls.** No step adds a retry against the same target, a loop, a background call, or a call an agent can relaunch. Only C45 adds cases that move a request to its alias's next target: a provider `401`, `402`, `403` or `404`, and an error object stating such a status, `429` or a `5xx` inside a `2xx` or before a stream's commit. C44's provider timers can make the existing move after a timeout come sooner. The cap is unchanged and in code: an alias has at most three targets (`internal/config/validate.go:505-506`) and `route` calls each at most once (`chat.go:390-408`), so one client request makes at most three provider calls. C45 also removes calls: an answer carries `x-should-retry: false` only when every target the request reached refused it for the client's or the operator's reason and none was passed over for a reason that time clears (an open circuit, a full quota window), so a retry would meet the same refusals; that stops the two default retries of the OpenAI and Anthropic SDKs (docs), each of which would be a new request of up to three calls. That a refused `401`/`402`/`403`/`404` is not billed is expected, since no generation ran; no provider's billing was checked (§14).

One cost moves rather than disappears. Any change of a provider declaration clears the stored verification of every target of that provider (RSN-9; `internal/config/service.go:186-197`, `internal/config/storage.go:274-282`), so a provider `params` or `timeouts` change brings back the `target_unverified` warning for each of its chat targets. The warning never blocks traffic (OPS-5); clearing it is `targets verify`, operator-ordered only, at `len(VerifyLevels)` calls per chat target (`internal/domain/verify.go:34-37`, `internal/api/verify.go:80`). §16 point 14 keeps the rule whole.

## 4. Provider knowledge has a home (root cause A)

**Root cause.** The unit of declaration is the alias; the vocabulary belongs to the provider. `Provider` and `Target` have no rules (`document.go:85-97`, `:122-132`); `TargetDefaults` carries only `dialect` and `output_cap_param` (`:116-119`); rules live in `Model.Params` (`:178`). `plan.Check` refuses an unknown key once per request with only the alias in hand, before any target is chosen (`plan.go:62-77`, called at `chat.go:325`). So every provider key (OpenRouter `provider` and `transforms`, vLLM and Ollama sampling keys, Mistral embeddings `output_dimension`, which kick-off §7.2 itself states, Anthropic keys newer than the pinned schema) is `400` for every alias until someone writes one rule per alias. A model's refusal can only skip the target (`plan.go:442`); removing the key needs a `drop` on every alias that reaches it (`:444-448`). The target's cap spelling applies only to an alias cap (`plan.go:358-374`): an OpenAI-SDK `max_tokens` reaches a target that declared `max_completion_tokens` unchanged (the `openai` preset, `internal/presets/presets.go:32`; docs: OpenAI reasoning models refuse it), and the reverse may reach servers that ignore the unknown name, uncapped (docs, not verified per server). Timers are read from the instance for every attempt (`chat.go:508`, `:742`; `internal/admin/verify.go:49`): in the comparison run one local alias hit the 180 s total timer while its successful calls took up to about 230 s.

Two facts already have the right home and only part of their effect is missing: a model's refusals live on the target (`unsupported_params`), only their removal needs alias rules; the cap spelling lives on the target, but applies to alias caps only. Timers live on the instance, not on the alias.

**Why one key at a time is a patch.** Adding seven keys to `internal/params/schema.go:43-45`, a README recipe ("add `--param x=forward` to each alias"), or a timeout block with its own resolver each closes one symptom; the next provider key is again a `400` for every alias.

### 4.1 The predicate

For each top-level key `k` of the body toward target `t` of provider `p`, with the endpoint's reference `s` and the translated path's `noEquivalent`, `plan.rules` decides in body order:

```go
// refuses is the one answer to "does t take k"; rules and the stream_options write (plan.go:388-389) read it.
func refuses(t *domain.Target, p *domain.Provider, k string) bool {
	return cmp.Or(t.Params[k], p.Params[k]) == drop || slices.Contains(t.UnsupportedParams, k)
}

fact, policy, known := cmp.Or(t.Params[k], p.Params[k]), m.Params[k], s.Known[k]
refused := refuses(t, p, k) || slices.Contains(noEquivalent, k)
switch {
case fact == drop || policy == drop && (refused || !known): // removed, listed k:dropped
case refused:                                             // skip t: 422 unsupported_parameter k if none is left
case known:                                               // a reference key, unchanged
case fact == forward || policy == forward:                // sent, listed k:forwarded
default:                                                  // skip t: no rule lets k through toward it
}
```

| Key | Target, else provider | Alias | Toward this target |
|---|---|---|---|
| reference key | nothing | nothing, or `drop` | unchanged (an alias `drop` acts only where a target refuses) |
| reference key | `drop` | anything | removed, `k:dropped` |
| reference key | `unsupported_params` | nothing / `drop` | skipped / removed |
| unknown key | `forward` | nothing or `forward` / `drop` | forwarded / removed (a stored alias `drop` still wins) |
| unknown key | `drop` | anything | removed |
| unknown key | nothing | `forward` / `drop` / nothing | forwarded / removed / skipped |
| chat key without equivalent toward `anthropic` | `forward` | nothing | skipped: no translation is invented |
| chat key without equivalent | `drop` at any level | — | removed |
| `stream_options` toward a `stream_usage: request` provider | `drop` or `unsupported_params` | — | the client's member removed or the target skipped, as for any key; the proxy does not write its own (the same `refuses`) |
| `tools`, `tool_choice`, `response_format`, `output_config`, `mcp_servers` | `drop` | — | not reachable: validation refuses the rule (PAR-4); the target declares the capability `false` and is skipped |

A target's rule replaces its provider's for the same key (a target `forward` over a provider `drop` forwards). Measured on a copy of `af8796c` carrying the two fields and this `rules`: `go test -race ./internal/... ./cmd/...` passes unchanged, so with no provider or target rule every body is today's; a 17-row table test of the cases above passes. The `stream_options` row and the extraction of `refuses` were not prototyped (derived).

### C43. A provider and its targets declare their vocabulary; the target's cap spelling reaches every cap

**Change.**
- `domain.Provider.Params`, `domain.Target.Params`: `map[string]string`, `json:"params,omitempty"`, values `forward` and `drop`.
- `plan.rules` (`plan.go:439-454`) is §4.1; its callers (`plan.go:326`, `:483`) pass the provider, so embeddings take provider rules too. The USE-8 write of `stream_options` (`plan.go:388-389`) asks `refuses` instead of reading `t.UnsupportedParams` itself, so `unsupported_params: [stream_options]` and `params: {stream_options: drop}` mean the same toward the proxy's own write.
- `config.Model.Declared map[string]bool`: every key of `Params` over the alias's published targets and their providers, filled in `build` (`internal/config/snapshot.go:220-228`). `plan.Check(ep, b, m, declared)`: the condition at `plan.go:72` becomes `!s.Known[k] && m.Params[k] == "" && !declared[k]`. Callers: `chat.go:325` and `internal/admin/explain.go:127` pass `Declared`; `internal/api/verify.go:96` passes nil (its body is the proxy's own and has no unknown key).
- The cap (`plan.go:358-374`): toward an `openai` target that declares `output_cap_param`, the client's cap members under other names are removed and the larger value the client sent (`*c.Cap`, PAR-6) is written under the declared name, listed `<client name>:translated`; the existing "every client cap member was dropped" case folds into it. An undeclared `output_cap_param` keeps today's bytes. A target that lists the cap name in `unsupported_params` keeps PAR-12: skipped unless a `drop` applies.
- Validation (`internal/config/validate.go`): one helper validates the values of the alias's, the provider's and the target's maps (replacing `:517-519`). `param_collision` (`:552-558`) also for a target `forward`, or a provider `forward` the target does not override, that names a key of `written(t, protocol)` (`:157-168`), reported on the resource that declares it. A target `forward` on a key the same target lists in `unsupported_params` is refused (`invalid_value`, PAR-12). A provider or target `drop` naming a contractual key, one of the top-level keys PAR-4 reads (`domain.ContractParams`: `tools`, `tool_choice`, `response_format`, `output_config`, and `mcp_servers` once C51 makes Messages detect it), is refused (`invalid_value`, PAR-4), the message naming the capability to declare `false` instead; the list sits in `domain` beside the capability names, so `config` gains no `params` import. An alias `drop` on such a key keeps its v1 meaning: it acts only toward a target that lists the key in `unsupported_params`, by the alias's own decision. A `drop` on a key the proxy writes is not a collision: the client's member is removed and the proxy writes its own value from the client's standard field or the alias's settings (`plan.go:331-332`), as PAR-2 says; for `stream_options` the proxy writes nothing toward a target that refuses it.
- CLI: `{"param", kindStringPairs, "params", …}` in `providerFlags` and `targetFlags` (`internal/cli/cli.go:106-118`, `:122-138`), as on models (`:147`).

## 5. One failure model (root cause B)

**Root cause.** `classify` (`chat.go:637-684`) is the one place that decides fallback, breaker and answer, which is right; its input and its table are wrong.
- It sees the status line, plus whichever typed error an adapter happens to raise. A native `2xx` body is an answer once it is a JSON object (`openai.go:28-68`, `:80-95`; `native.go:28-70`). OpenRouter documents a `200` whose body is only `{"error":{…,"code":502}}` "even when every provider fails" (docs): it is relayed as success, the breaker hears `Pass` (`chat.go:669-670`), and the provider's text reaches the client inside a `200` (measured (review)). An Anthropic-compatible `200 {"type":"error",…}` is relayed the same way (derived: `native.go:28-70` accepts any JSON object).
- Two in-stream error types with two rules: an Anthropic event is classified by its type's status (`chat.go:650-654`); an OpenAI chunk carrying the same numeric code is never eligible (`:655-656`; `internal/adapters/openai/stream.go:46-53`, `:90-92`).
- The table asks "is this a 4xx?", not "who must act?". `401` and `403` pass the breaker and never fall back (`chat.go:671-672`); `402` and every unlisted status do the same (`:683`); a `404` is returned with the provider's message (`:673-675`), although the proxy wrote the URL and the model id (`plan.go:356`, `:418-423`). The same fault detected locally goes the other way: a credential that does not resolve passes the target over (`chat.go:456-458`).
- Each consequence has its own reader: the client text from one shape (`chat.go:804-812`), unbounded up to `max_response_bytes`, also on the WARN line (`:588-589`) and the attempt's cause (`:846-848`); the WARN only for `upstream_auth_error` and only on the synchronous path (`:584-591`); the span message with its own status list (`:1018`); the record by "is the wire status `2xx`" (`internal/usage/usage.go:116`).

Consequences: an alias whose first provider is out of credit answers `502` on every request although a healthy second target exists; an OpenRouter outage is invisible to RES-1 and RES-2; SDKs retry a deterministic `502` twice (docs).

**Why adding cases is a patch.** Keeping `classify`'s input and adding a third error case for synchronous bodies, a fourth for chunks, a header rule and a message rule would give each its own exception list. Here the adapters report one type, `classify` turns it into a status in one branch, one table runs for every source, and every consumer reads the verdict instead of re-deriving it.

### 5.1 The model

An attempt ends in one of: **answered** (a usable `2xx`, complete or cut after the commit); **no verdict** (the client gone; the shutdown, `500 server stopping`); **a fault with an effective status** `s`, the status line or, when the provider put an error object in place of an answer, the status that object states; **a fault without a status** (a timer, a transport failure, a cut, a `2xx` that is neither an answer nor a coded error). One table gives every consequence:

| Class | Faults | Fallback | Breaker | Client, when no target is left | Verdict `final` | Operator log | Record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transient: time may fix it | timer, transport failure, cut, `429`, any `5xx` | yes | Fail | `504 upstream_timeout` for a timer; `429` with its `Retry-After`; else `502 upstream_error` | no | attempt line, with the provider's text | as today; cost per C49 when nothing answered |
| The operator's: the account, model id or URL the target declares | `401`, `402`, `403`, `404` | yes | Pass | `502 upstream_auth_error` (`401`, `403`) or `502 upstream_error` (`402`, `404`), fixed message | yes | attempt line, with the provider's text; a rate-limited WARN as the alert | zero tokens, priced 0 |
| The client's: the request | `400`, `413`, `422` | no | Pass | the provider's status and message, redacted, cut to 2,000 characters | yes | attempt line | zero tokens, priced 0 |
| Nobody can tell | a `2xx` not usable (it may have been billed), a `3xx`, any other status, an error object without a status | no | Pass | `502 upstream_error` | no | attempt line, with the provider's text | as today |

**The provider's text.** It is what the extractor reads from a non-`2xx` body or from an error object; an unusable `2xx` without one has none, so no part of an answer reaches the log (OPS-9). Wherever the client does not get it (every class but the client's), the bounded, redacted text is on that attempt's line, so each refusal is visible whatever the WARN's limiter dropped; the WARN, one per 10 s across targets (`internal/api/api.go:28`, `:81`), stays the alert, not the record.

**The retry hint is the request's, not the attempt's.** `final` says this attempt's refusal would repeat: the client's request is refused for its content, or the operator's declaration is refused. A request is final, and its answer carries `x-should-retry: false`, only when every attempt made was final and no target was passed over at step 5 of kick-off §14.1 for what can change between requests (an open circuit, which reopens after 20 s, `routing.go:15`; a quota or budget window). Only `route` sees all of that (`chat.go:390-411`), so it sets `Error.Final`; `classify` stays the only place that classes an attempt. "Nobody can tell" is not final: a provider `408`, `409` or a one-off malformed `2xx` is retried by the SDKs today and keeps being retried.

**One exception, stated rather than coded.** In-band detection reads an answer in the shape the request asked for. A `2xx` that answers a stream request with a body that is not an event stream is classified without reading it (`chat.go:705-708`): "nobody can tell", as today, whatever error object it holds. No provider documents that answer and none was seen; reading it would be code for a case nobody observed.

The breaker counts only faults that say the target is unhealthy whatever the request. The operator's class does not meet that test on every provider: a `403` can be a moderation refusal of one client's input, a `402` can compare the remaining credit with the cap one request asked for, and a `404` can mean "no endpoint supports the feature this request uses" (docs: OpenRouter, not re-fetched). Counting them would let one client open a circuit for every client (`routing.go:13-15`), and an open circuit on a single-target alias answers `503 no_target_available` (`chat.go:459-461`), which SDKs retry and which hides the `upstream_auth_error` the operator needs to see. The cost of not counting is one refused call, expected not to be billed, before the fallback on each request; the WARN makes the fault visible. §16 point 1 keeps counting `401` alone as the owner's choice.

## 7. A response ends for the client when its request ends (root cause D)

**Root cause.** A synchronous answer is written into the server's buffer (`chat.go:576`) and reaches the client when the handler returns, after `done` has released the slots (`chat.go:223`, `:952-956`). A stream flushes every event, the last one included (`:722-725`); the record, the charge, the attempt line, the spans, the request line and only then the release follow (`:822-867`, `:973-986`). A sequential client that starts its next request on `data: [DONE]` can arrive before the release and get a spurious `429` from its own `max_parallel_requests: 1`. Releasing the slot early for streams only would be asymmetric and leave a residual window; the symmetric fix holds the end of the stream as the end of a synchronous answer is already held.

### C47. The end of a stream leaves when the handler returns

**Change.** `write` (`chat.go:711-733`) no longer flushes. The read loop (`:745-770`) flushes after each upstream event it relayed while nothing ended the stream. What the adapter emits for the upstream event that ends it stays in the server's buffer and leaves when the handler returns, after `finish` (the charge, LIM-4) and `done` (the release): on the native paths the terminator alone (`[DONE]`, `message_stop`); on the translated path the finish-reason chunk, the usage chunk and `[DONE]`, which `ChatStream.Event` emits for the one `message_stop` (`internal/adapters/anthropic/stream.go:157-172`); with them any think-tag text a stripper held back until the end (`internal/adapters/openai/stream.go:77-84`, `internal/adapters/anthropic/native.go:151-158`); and the RES-4 terminal error written at `:798-799`. About 12 lines.

**Behaviour.**

| Log sink delay; key at `max_parallel_requests: 1`; 2,000 sequential streams over two warm connections | `af8796c` | C47 |
|---|---|---|
| none | 2 spurious `429`s (measured; 2, 3, 2 in three runs of the review) | 0 (measured) |
| 200 µs | 1,000 (measured) | 0 (measured) |
| 2 ms | 1,000 (measured (review)) | 0 (measured (review)) |
| synchronous requests, control | 0 | 0 |

The output of every earlier upstream event is flushed as today (RES-4: nothing is buffered beyond the event being relayed); on the native paths that includes the finish-reason and usage chunks. The held output waits for in-memory bookkeeping, microseconds with a fast log sink; a slow sink delays it as it already delays every synchronous answer. A client that leaves between its last content and the terminator is recorded complete rather than `client_gone`. The fix removes the race for clients that read to the terminator, and on the translated path also for clients that stop at the finish reason; a client that starts its next request on a native `finish_reason` chunk, before reading `[DONE]` or `message_stop`, still races, and nobody measured how many do (§14).

**Tests.** `TestStreamEndsAfterRequest` (new, deterministic, on the three relays: chat native, messages native, chat translated): the log hook blocks on the request line; while it is blocked the client has received every content chunk, and on the native paths the finish-reason chunk, and none of the held output (the terminator; on the translated path also the finish-reason and usage chunks); unblocked, the held output arrives. It fails on `af8796c`, where the terminator is flushed before the request line; the content chunks arriving while blocked is the control on both. Measured on a copy of `af8796c` with the flush prototype: exactly that split on both paths, and the full suite passes with `-race`. Controls: the terminal error event is written once (`TestChatStreamFaults`); `TestBudgetChargeOrder`, `TestParallelLimits`, `TestSlotReleasedOnPanic` unchanged. **Kick-off.** K14. **Net code.** `+8 / −4`; tests about `+60`. **Effort** S. **Depends on** nothing.

## 8. A transport sized for a proxy (root cause E)

**Root cause.** `transport.NewClient` builds an `http.Transport` from zero values (`internal/transport/client.go:30-34`): two idle connections per host (Go's `DefaultMaxIdleConnsPerHost`) and no idle timeout. A proxy concentrates many agents on a handful of hosts (every OpenRouter alias shares one). At 64 calls in flight against a local mock, 9,630 new connections per 20,000 requests; against a TLS mock, 198 handshakes for 200 calls against 100 with a sized pool (measured (review)); the v1 benchmark showed about 3,000 sockets in `TIME_WAIT` per throughput run (`docs/benchmark.md:106-122`). Each new connection is also a lookup (`client.go:39-44`).

### C48. The idle pool fits the traffic

**Change.** In `NewClient` only: `MaxIdleConnsPerHost: 100`, `IdleConnTimeout: 90 * time.Second` (the value of Go's `DefaultTransport`, as hygiene for up to 100 idle connections per host; no provider's idle time is known). Both outbound clients use it (`cmd/stargaite/serve.go:189`). SEC-4's checks (`client.go:48-52`, `:73-81`) are untouched. This reverses the decision recorded in `docs/benchmark.md:106-122` ("`transport.NewClient` is unchanged", taken on loopback, where churn cost nothing measurable); the pull request rewrites that paragraph with the TLS handshake figure and the second-wave figure below.

**Behaviour.** The second wave of parallel calls reuses the pool (measured (review): 16 then 14 new connections on `af8796c`, 16 then 0 with a sized pool). Over a real network each reused connection saves a lookup and a TLS handshake; no figure was measured over a WAN. A kept-alive connection the provider closed stays an ordinary transport failure: transient, eligible, counted (§15). The dial loop (`client.go:53-61`) keeps its order and its single 60 s backstop; a per-address budget for a silently dropping first address is §16 point 8.

**Tests.** `TestClientPool`: two waves of 16 against a counting listener; the second opens 0; and the transport's `IdleConnTimeout` is 90 s. **Kick-off.** None. **Net code.** `+3`; tests about `+50`. **Effort** S. **Depends on** nothing.

## 16. Open points for the owner

Each is a genuine choice with a recommendation; the design above already assumes it.

1. **The breaker on the operator's class.** `Pass` for `401`, `402`, `403` and `404` (recommended: each can depend on one request on at least one provider, and an open circuit turns `upstream_auth_error` into `503 no_target_available` on a single-target alias). Alternative: `Fail` for `401` alone, which stops a revoked key costing one refused call per request after five, at the price of that `503`.
2. **The client's answer for a provider `404`.** `502 upstream_error` with a fixed text, the provider's text on the attempt line (recommended: the client usually cannot act on it, and a relayed `404` reads like the proxy's own `model_not_found`). Against it: on some providers a `404`, `403` or `402` depends on the request (§5.1), and then the hidden text was the client's to read. Alternative: relay the provider's `404` and text once no target is left.
3. **`user` and `safety_identifier` toward `anthropic`.** No equivalent in this phase (recommended): one provider-level `drop` serves them, listed; no §24 amendment; a stored alias `user: drop` keeps its meaning. Alternative: map them to `metadata.user_id`, which needs a §24 clarification, rewrites the `TestTranslated` row at `internal/plan/plan_test.go:468-470` (an alias `user: drop` would stop removing `user` toward `anthropic`, since a key with an equivalent is accepted) and a release note.
4. **`name` on user, assistant and system messages.** Untranslatable until `param` on the request line shows demand (recommended), or redundant and not sent.
5. **`image_url.detail`.** The fifth outcome, advisory, with its criterion in §7.1 and `detail` its only member (recommended: the image is sent whole and the target decides its resolution).
